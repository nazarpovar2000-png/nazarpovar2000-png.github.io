How Much Does a Deck Cost in Everett, WA?

A realistic 2026 breakdown of deck costs in Everett, WA: materials, size, railing, stairs, and what drives the price up or down.

If you have priced a deck recently, you already know the range people quote is useless: somewhere between "a few thousand" and "more than a car." Here is what deck work actually costs in the Everett area, and what moves the number.

What a deck actually costs

In the Everett and Snohomish County market, most residential decks land between roughly $35 and $75 per square foot installed. For a typical 12x16 foot deck (192 square feet), that is about $6,700 at the low end and $14,400 at the high end.

Three things explain almost all of that spread: material, height off the ground, and railing.

Material is the biggest lever

Pressure-treated pine is the budget choice. It is strong and cheap, but in our climate it needs staining every two to three years and will still grey and check over time.

Cedar costs more up front, looks better longer, and resists rot naturally — but it still needs maintenance.

Composite (Trex, TimberTech and similar) runs 30 to 60 percent more in material cost, but needs nothing beyond an occasional wash. In western Washington, where moss and moisture are constant, many homeowners find the maintenance savings justify it.

Height and access change the framing cost

A deck 18 inches off the ground is mostly decking and railing. A deck eight feet up needs longer posts, deeper footings, cross-bracing, and usually engineered stairs. That structural work can add several thousand dollars on its own.

Access matters too. If a truck cannot reach the backyard, everything gets carried — and that shows up in labor.

Railing and stairs

Wood railing is the cheapest option. Composite matches the deck and costs more. Metal and cable rail cost the most but open up the view and last decades.

Stairs are priced almost separately. A simple straight run is usually $1,500 to $3,500; anything with a landing, a turn, or more than six feet of rise goes higher.

What is usually included

A fair deck quote should include footings and concrete, framing and joist hangers, ledger flashing against the house, decking, railing, stairs, fasteners, and haul-away of old material. If a quote does not mention flashing, ask about it — that is the single most common source of hidden rot.

Permits

Decks above a certain height, or attached to the house, usually need a permit through the City of Everett or Snohomish County. Permit fees vary but typically run a few hundred dollars, and the review can add a couple of weeks before work starts.

Ways to spend less without regretting it

Keep the deck close to the ground, keep the shape rectangular, use pressure-treated framing even if you use composite decking, and skip the built-in bench and planter details for now. Those can be added later.

What not to do: skip the flashing, skip the footings, or use untreated wood for anything touching soil. Those are the shortcuts that turn a ten-year deck into a five-year deck.

Frequently asked questions

What is the cheapest way to build a deck?

Pressure-treated decking and framing, a simple rectangular shape, low height, and wood railing. That combination typically lands near the $35 per square foot end of the range.

Is composite decking worth it in Washington?

For many homeowners, yes. Composite costs more up front but needs no staining, which matters in a climate that destroys wood finishes quickly.

How long does a deck last in Everett?

A properly built and maintained wood deck lasts 15 to 25 years. Composite decking typically lasts 25 to 30. The framing detail matters more than the decking material.
