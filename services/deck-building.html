Deck building in Everett, WA

Custom decks, rebuilds and repairs built for Northwest rain — composite or cedar, with stairs and railings that pass inspection.

A deck in Western Washington lives a hard life. Nine months of rain, moss in the shade, and summers that dry the boards out until they cup and split. Most of the decks we replace around Everett were not failing because the homeowner waited too long — they were failing because the original build skipped the details that matter here: proper footing depth, galvanized hardware, joist flashing, and gaps that actually let water off the surface.

We build decks the way they should have been built the first time. That starts with checking what is under the existing structure — footing size, ledger attachment, rot at the rim joist — before we quote anything. If the frame is sound, we can often keep it and replace the surface, railings and stairs for a fraction of a full rebuild. If it is not, we will show you exactly why, with photos, before recommending a rebuild.

Composite (Trex, TimberTech and similar) is what most homeowners choose now: no annual staining, no splinters, consistent color. Cedar still has its fans for the look and the feel underfoot, and it is the better choice for some covered porches and heritage homes. We install both, and we will tell you honestly which one fits your site, your shade and your budget.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a permit for a deck in Everett?

Usually yes for a new deck or a structural rebuild. Everett and Snohomish County require a permit for decks more than 30 inches above grade, and most attached decks need one regardless. We handle the paperwork and inspections as part of the project.

Composite or cedar — which is better here?

Composite costs more up front but needs no staining and holds up better in our wet climate. Cedar is beautiful and repairable, but it needs cleaning and resealing every one to two years. In deep shade, composite wins on maintenance; under a covered porch, cedar is a fine choice.

Can you replace just the boards and keep the frame?

Often yes. If the joists, ledger and footings are sound and dry, a surface-and-railing replacement is the smartest money you can spend. We check with a probe and a level before recommending it.

How long does a deck take?

A typical 12×16 composite deck is three to five working days once materials are on site. Permit review adds one to three weeks before we start.

Do you work in the rain?

Framing yes, most finish work no. Composite can be cut wet; staining and some trim work need dry conditions. We plan around the forecast rather than fighting it.
