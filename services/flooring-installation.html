Flooring installation

Luxury vinyl plank, laminate, tile and hardwood — installed over a subfloor we actually check and fix first.

Every flooring failure we get called about starts in the same place: the subfloor. A floor that clicks, bounces, or shows a ridge along a seam was almost always laid over a hump or a soft spot that nobody measured. We check flatness with a straightedge and a laser before the first box is opened, and we fix what we find — grinding high spots, filling low ones, replacing water-damaged plywood — instead of covering it up.

Luxury vinyl plank is the most common choice in this area now, and for good reason: it handles moisture, pets and kids, and it is comfortable underfoot in a way tile is not. Laminate is still a good budget option for dry rooms. Engineered hardwood and solid hardwood are what we install when the house deserves it. We also do tile in bathrooms, entries and kitchens, with the waterproofing done properly underneath.

We remove and dispose of the old floor, move what needs moving, and handle transitions and stair nosing so the details look intentional. Baseboards go back on clean, and we leave you the leftover material for future repairs.

Frequently asked questions

Do you move furniture?

We move furniture and appliances as part of the job — everything except very large items like a piano or a loaded gun safe, which we ask you to arrange. We put things back at the end of the day where possible.

Can LVP go over existing tile?

Sometimes, if the tile is sound, flat and fully bonded, and the manufacturer allows it. Floating a floor over failing tile is a mistake. We test for hollow spots and tell you which way is safe.

How long before I can walk on it?

LVP and laminate are walkable immediately. Tile needs 24 hours before foot traffic and longer before heavy furniture. Glue-down hardwood needs about 24 hours.

Do you install over concrete slabs?

Yes, with a moisture test first. Slabs in older Everett homes can wick moisture, and the wrong underlayment will trap it and ruin the floor.

How much extra material should I buy?

Plan on 10 percent waste for a simple rectangular room and 15 percent for rooms with lots of angles, closets or a diagonal layout. Keep the leftovers.
